OSS from 2027: sales from stock in another EU country no longer count towards the €10,000 threshold — a 5-step check for sellers with stock in Spain

2026-11-26 · IberTrust Global

A typical scenario

A small homeware brand is registered in Italy and ships most orders from its own warehouse near Milan. Its sales to consumers in other EU countries from that warehouse come to about €8,000 a year, so it charges Italian VAT on them: it is below the EU's €10,000 threshold. Last spring it also sent pallets to a fulfilment centre in Spain to deliver faster to Spanish and Portuguese customers. Those orders went onto the same invoices, with the same Italian VAT. “We're under the threshold,” the founder says. The question that matters is a different one: where did each parcel start its journey?

From 1 January 2027, EU law answers that question in black and white. Here is what changes, what does not, and a five-step check you can run on your own sales data before the new year.

What changes on 1 January 2027

The €10,000 threshold in Article 59c of the VAT Directive lets a business established in a single EU country keep charging its home VAT on cross-border sales to consumers (intra-EU distance sales of goods, plus telecom, broadcasting and electronic services) as long as the total stays within €10,000 in the current and the previous calendar year. Council Directive (EU) 2025/516, the “VAT in the Digital Age” (ViDA) package, amends that article from 1 January 2027 in two ways:

  • Only goods dispatched from the country where you are established count. Sales shipped from stock held in another Member State are left out of the calculation. They follow the normal rule: VAT of the country where the transport ends.
  • Registering for the Union OSS counts as opting out of the threshold. Once you are in the OSS Union scheme, you are deemed to have chosen destination taxation, and that option covers at least two calendar years.

The European Commission's revised Explanatory Notes point out that the first change writes into law a guideline the EU VAT Committee had already agreed and that was already applied in practice. In other words, the Italian brand in our scenario does not have a 2027 problem; it already has one. What 2027 removes is the room to argue.

Who is affected, and how

EU seller with stock in another country

Sales from that stock sit outside the threshold. Sales to consumers in a third country are taxed there (through OSS or a local VAT number); sales to consumers in the stock country are domestic sales there.

Seller established outside the EU

The threshold never applied to you. You can use the Union OSS for distance sales from EU stock; your OSS country is the one the goods ship from. Marketplaces are the deemed supplier for sales they facilitate from your EU stock.

Shipping from outside the EU

The IOSS covers imported consignments up to €150. From 2027 it cannot be combined with the EU SME exemption scheme. The €150 cap is due to go from 1 July 2028.

Shipping only from your home country

Nothing changes in substance: add up all cross-border consumer sales of goods and electronic services from your home country, this year and last.

A five-step check on your own sales

  1. 1Map every dispatch point

    Own warehouse, 3PL, marketplace fulfilment network (including stock a programme moves for you), and suppliers shipping from outside the EU.

  2. 2Tag each consumer order by origin

    Home country, EU stock elsewhere, or outside the EU. Use where the parcel left from, not which company issued the invoice.

  3. 3Test the threshold on home dispatches only

    Cross-border consumer sales from home plus electronic services, this year and last. Over €10,000, or registered for OSS: destination VAT.

  4. 4Treat foreign stock separately

    Holding stock in another EU country normally requires a VAT registration there. Local sales go on that return; cross-border sales are taxed at destination.

  5. 5Check who declares what

    Marketplace sales may be declared by the platform as deemed supplier; your own webshop sales are yours. Note the two-year OSS lock-in and keep evidence per order.

Run the five steps on the Italian brand and the picture is clear. Its €8,000 shipped from Milan can stay under Italian VAT while it remains outside OSS. Its Portuguese orders from Spain carry Portuguese VAT. Its Spanish orders from Spain are Spanish domestic sales. None of the Spanish-stock orders should have carried Italian VAT.

If the stock is in Spain

  • Spanish registration. A business that holds goods in Spain will normally need a Spanish NIF and VAT registration (Modelo 036) and will report its sales made in Spain on Spanish VAT returns.
  • Your OSS does not move to Spain if you are EU-established. The OSS country is your country of establishment, so the Italian brand declares its Portuguese orders from Spanish stock in its Italian OSS return.
  • Sellers from outside the EU whose goods ship from Spain can choose Spain as their OSS country and file the Spanish OSS return (Modelo 369) with the Spanish Tax Agency (AEAT). If goods ship from several EU countries, the choice binds for the current year and the two following.
  • Input VAT stays out of OSS. Spanish VAT paid on costs in Spain cannot be recovered through an OSS return; it goes through the Spanish VAT return or the refund procedure.

Three misreadings we expect to see in January

  1. “We are small, so the threshold covers everything.” It only covers what you ship from home.
  2. “OSS means we do not need a Spanish VAT number.” OSS covers cross-border consumer sales. It does not cover your domestic sales in Spain from Spanish stock, or your Spanish input VAT.
  3. “The IOSS €150 limit has already gone.” On 9 November 2026 EU countries reached a general approach in the Council on removing it. The Commission gives 1 July 2028 as the date, once the EU Customs Data Hub is operational, and the European Parliament is being consulted again before formal adoption. Until then, consignments above €150 stay outside IOSS.

FAQ

Does the €10,000 threshold apply to a company established outside the EU?

No. It requires establishment in a single EU Member State. Sellers established outside the EU, or in more than one Member State, charge VAT of the destination country on intra-EU distance sales from the first euro.

We are established in one EU country and also hold stock in Spain. What counts towards the threshold?

From 1 January 2027, only cross-border consumer sales dispatched from your country of establishment, plus any telecom, broadcasting and electronic services. Sales from your Spanish stock are excluded and are taxed where the goods arrive.

Can we register for OSS for just a few months?

Registering for the Union OSS is treated as opting for destination taxation, and that option covers at least two calendar years. Do not plan on switching back to home-country VAT under the threshold within that period.

Can we declare stock transfers through OSS in 2027?

No. The transfer-of-own-goods module belongs to the Single VAT Registration phase that the Commission schedules from 1 July 2028. In 2027, stock held in another EU country normally still requires a VAT registration there.
