The representative’s Spanish NIF: why it is required, and why the person does not have to be a director

2026-11-24 · IberTrust Global

When a foreign company applies for a Spanish NIF, the AEAT does not only identify the company. It also identifies the person who acts for it. In the published guidance, the legal representatives of a foreign legal entity that carries out transactions with tax implications in Spain must have a Spanish NIF. If that person has no NIE, the number assigned is usually a NIF starting with the letter M.

This is a frequent sticking point. The company’s documents may be ready, but the census form cannot be completed properly until the representative has a Spanish tax identification number. A passport or a tax number from the home country is not a substitute.

How the representative obtains a NIF

The AEAT explains that a foreign legal representative of a foreign legal entity can apply:

  • at an AEAT office, on Modelo 030; or
  • through a Spanish diplomatic mission or consular office abroad.

The company’s NIF is requested separately, on Modelo 036. In practice the two filings are prepared together, because the 036 must identify the representative and, where the representative is required to have a Spanish NIF, that number has to be available. We set out the company application in How a foreign company gets a Spanish NIF.

The NIF M is definitive for foreign nationals who live in Spain but are not required to have a NIE. It is only temporary where the person is required to obtain a NIE; in that case it generally lasts until the NIE is issued, with a usual validity of three months.

The representative does not have to be a director

Spanish tax rules distinguish between legal representation and voluntary representation.

  • A legal person acts through the persons who, at the relevant time, hold the offices that the law or a validly adopted resolution have given the power of representation — typically the directors named in the constitution documents or the articles.
  • Voluntary representation may be granted, where the content of their authority allows it, by those who already hold the legal representation. It can be conferred on a natural person or on a legal person.

The title of representation recorded on Modelo 036 can be a public document (including a notarial power of attorney, or the deed of incorporation or the articles if they already appoint the person), a private document with or without a notarised signature, an appearance before the administrative body, or another accepted form.

For the document that proves the signer’s authority, official consular and AEAT guidance points to either:

  • a certificate from the registry or tax authority of the home country showing that the signer is the legal representative; or
  • a notarial power of attorney granted by the legal representative.

A board resolution that validly appoints a representative, or a notarial power, can therefore be used when the person who will deal with the AEAT is not a director. The document must be sufficient, and if it was issued outside Spain it will usually need the same authenticity and translation formalities as the rest of the foreign file — unless an official exception applies. Those formalities are explained in our guide to apostille and sworn translation.

This representative on the census form is not automatically the same person as a fiscal representative for VAT. Non-EU businesses with VAT obligations in Spain must generally appoint a representative with a tax domicile in Spain, with limited exceptions. That is a separate appointment, described in NIF vs VAT vs EORI vs OSS.

Common reasons the representative’s file is delayed or sent back

  • The company file is submitted before the representative has a Spanish NIF.
  • A passport or a foreign tax number is offered instead of a Spanish NIF, which the AEAT has stated is not valid for this purpose.
  • The power of attorney is missing, is not signed by someone who can grant it, or names a different person from the one who signs Modelo 036.
  • The constitution documents do not identify the signer as legal representative, and no separate power or resolution is filed.
  • A foreign power of attorney is filed without an apostille or consular legalisation, or without a translation, in a case where those formalities are still required.
  • Identity documents of the representative are missing or do not match the census form.

FAQ

Must the representative be a company director?

No. A director named in the constitution documents can act as legal representative without a separate power. Someone who is not a director can be appointed by a valid board resolution or by a notarial power of attorney granted by the legal representative, provided the document is sufficient.

Does the representative have to live in Spain?

For the census identification of the company, the published AEAT guidance requires a Spanish NIF for the foreign legal representative; it does not say that this person must reside in Spain. A fiscal representative for VAT, where one is required, must have a tax domicile in Spain. The two roles should not be confused.

Can the representative’s NIF and the company’s NIF be filed at the same time?

Yes. That is how we normally prepare the file: the representative’s NIF and the company’s NIF — and VAT registration where needed — are submitted together.

How long does the representative’s NIF take?

There is no single published deadline that covers every NIF M application. In the files we prepare together with the company’s NIF, the numbers are usually issued in about three to six weeks. The actual timing is set by the AEAT.
