A Spanish consumer authority writes to the EU Responsible Person on your listing: the 10-day clock, the evidence it wants, and why a name without a mandate backfires

2026-11-17 · IberTrust Global

The notice did not arrive by email. It appeared in the electronic notification inbox of the company named as EU Responsible Person on a marketplace listing for a charging cable. A regional consumer inspection office in Spain had already received one reply. Now it wanted more: complete, legible copies, in date order, of every email exchanged that month with the manufacturer, suppliers or intermediaries about safety alerts, withdrawals, technical specifications or conformity. Deadline: ten working days.

We prepared that reply. The twist: there was no mandate for that product at all. The name had been put on the listing without authorisation. For non-EU sellers, the case shows what Spanish authorities now ask the responsible person named on your listing, and what happens when that person cannot stand behind your product.

Who writes, and why now

In Spain, the consumer authorities of the autonomous communities carry out product safety inspections, coordinated by the Directorate-General for Consumer Affairs. Spain’s national report on the 2026 EU product safety sweep shows how the responsible person is checked. Authorities contacted the responsible person named on online offers and gave them 10 calendar days to confirm four things:

  1. that it really is the responsible person for that product;
  2. that its contact details also appear on the physical product;
  3. that the product is the one described in the technical documentation;
  4. documented evidence of the periodic checks required by Article 16(2) GPSR.

The results for the 67 offers checked in Spain were poor. Responsible persons were verified for 66% of the offers. Replies within ten days came for 33%, confirmation of the role for 22%, confirmation of details on the product for 19%, and documented evidence of the checks for 15%. Authorities have taken or plan measures on at least 18 offers, including formal requirements, and do not rule out sanction proceedings for failing to answer.

What the authority asks, and what your representative needs from you

A responsible person can only answer with what the manufacturer has given it. Under Article 4(3) of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, which Article 16(1) GPSR applies to consumer products, the responsible person must provide the authority, on a reasoned request, with all the information and documents needed to show the product conforms.

How the clock runs

Spanish administrative deadlines follow Law 39/2015 unless a specific rule says otherwise:

  • Notification date. An electronic notification takes effect when its content is accessed. Where electronic notification is compulsory, it counts as rejected if it is not opened within 10 calendar days of being made available (Article 43). Not opening it does not stop the procedure.
  • Working days. Deadlines in days are working days. Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays are excluded, unless the notice expressly says calendar days (Article 30(2)).
  • Start. Counting starts on the day after notification (Article 30(3)).
  • Holidays. Check national, regional and local holidays. The sweep emails used 10 calendar days, so read each notice.

In the case above, the notice was opened on a Monday, so day one was Tuesday. With no holidays in between, the tenth working day fell on the Monday two weeks later. The authority also warned that a late or inaccurate answer, or false documents, is an infringement under regional consumer law, and that repeated refusal can be classed as serious or very serious.

When the name on the listing has no mandate

A responsible person without a written mandate for the product will tell the authority so in writing. The reply we prepared set out the facts and attached an earlier written notice telling a former seller to stop using the responsible person’s details, plus the reports sent to a marketplace. About a week after the report, that marketplace said it had decided to remove the listing.

For the seller, the result is worse than having no representative. The authority now has a written statement that no EU operator stands behind the product, and under Article 16(1) GPSR it cannot be placed on the market. The same happens when a contract has ended but old listings or labels still carry the representative’s name.

Step by step: be ready before the letter

  1. Sign a written mandate that names the legal entity behind each shop and lists the brands or models.
  2. Match label and listing. Show the same responsible person, with the same postal and electronic address, on the product, packaging or accompanying document and in every listing.
  3. Keep a product file per model: technical documentation, test reports, photos of labels and instructions in the language of the country of sale.
  4. Record the periodic checks your representative performs, and answer its check requests promptly.
  5. Keep safety emails in one thread per product, so a complete chain can be exported in date order.
  6. Agree who answers and how fast. Your representative may need documents from you within days, not weeks.
  7. When you change representative, update listings on every marketplace and the labels on new stock at the same time.

Common mistakes

  • Copying a representative’s details from another seller or an old listing.
  • Leaving the former representative on listings after the contract ends.
  • A mandate signed by one company while the shop belongs to another.
  • Sending the representative partial email threads or screenshots instead of complete chains.
  • Counting the deadline in calendar days when the notice says working days, or the other way round.

Who this does not apply to

  • Manufacturers established in the EU: they are the responsible person themselves.
  • Products placed on the market by an EU importer whose details appear on the product. The authority writes to that importer.
  • Products outside the scope of the GPSR, such as medicines or food.
  • Cosmetics and medical devices, which have their own responsible person or authorised representative rules.

FAQ

Does an authority letter mean my product is unsafe?

Not necessarily. In the 2026 sweep, authorities contacted responsible persons to check that the information on offers was true. A letter is a check, but an unanswered one can lead to formal measures.

Can my representative answer without my help?

Only partly. It can confirm its role and its own checks. Technical documentation, test reports and emails with suppliers usually have to come from the manufacturer.

What happens if my listing names a representative I never signed with?

That representative will tell the authority in writing that it has no mandate and may report the listing to the marketplace. The product then has no EU operator behind it, and the listing can be removed.

Is the deadline in calendar days or working days?

Read the notice. Under Law 39/2015, days are working days unless the notice says calendar days. Counting starts the day after notification.
