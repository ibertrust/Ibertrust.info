Pre-sold parcels on one pallet: why EU customs can still charge the €3 duty and €2 handling fee per item — distance sale vs genuine bulk import

2026-11-16 · IberTrust Global

Picture a seller who ships small home-goods orders from Asia straight to customers in Spain, France and Italy. Since July every parcel under €150 has carried a new customs duty, and since 1 November a second charge. A logistics agent suggests a fix: “Put the week’s orders, already labelled for each customer, on one pallet. We declare it as one bulk import into a Spanish warehouse. One declaration, one line, one charge.”

It sounds efficient. It is also, almost word for word, the pattern the European Commission’s customs guidance tells customs authorities to look for. Here is how the charges are counted, where a distance sale ends and a genuine bulk import begins, and what to check first.

The two charges

Temporary €3 customs duty. Since 1 July 2026, Council Regulation (EU) 2026/382 has replaced the old €150 duty exemption with a flat duty of €3 per item in consignments worth up to €150 (intrinsic value) sold in distance sales. It applies whatever VAT scheme is used (IOSS, special arrangements or standard import VAT) and is scheduled to run until 1 July 2028.

Union handling fee. Since 1 November 2026, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/2152 has set a fee of €2 per item for releasing goods sold in distance sales. It is not limited to consignments up to €150, it is not temporary, and it is not refunded once customs has accepted the declaration.

Both are owed by the declarant (the seller, the platform or their customs representative), not by the consumer. Under indirect representation, the person represented is a debtor too.

What counts as one “item”

An item is not one physical unit. It is the goods in a consignment that share the same tariff classification, description and, where the declaration asks for it, origin. In practice both charges are applied per declaration line, whatever the quantity on it, and goods with different tariff codes may not be grouped under one line.

The Commission’s own example: a €140 parcel with three women’s suits in different fabrics is one line in an H7 or H6 declaration, but three lines in H1, because each fabric has its own TARIC code. The same parcel can carry one charge or three.

The pallet idea: what customs looks at

A distance sale needs a business seller, an EU customer who is not a VAT-registered business, transport arranged by or on behalf of the seller (even indirectly), and goods that are outside the EU when the sale takes place. That last point decides most cases. If the customer has already ordered while the goods are still outside the EU, it is a distance sale, however the goods travel afterwards.

The Commission’s guidance (version of 5 October 2026) adds three points:

  • Orders of two consumers that are to be delivered separately are always two consignments and need two declarations.
  • Goods sold to consumers before being stored in a customs warehouse for final distribution count as dispatched from outside the EU, so both charges apply. An amendment covering goods sold while in storage is being prepared.
  • Under the anti-abuse clause (Article 243(5) of the UCC Implementing Regulation), where goods pass through a chain of sales before entering the EU and one of them is a distance sale, customs considers only the distance sale.

Indicators include parcels with their own label, barcode or QR code pointing to different destinations, how often the operator files such declarations, and the “buyer” field in H1. If customs concludes a “bulk” declaration was really a group of distance sales, it can recalculate per item, ask for a guarantee, or invite you to replace the declaration, without IOSS. Where a customs debt arises through non-compliance, the operator must prove the import was not linked to a distance sale.

Step by step before you change your logistics

  1. Map each channel: when the sale happens and where the goods are at that moment.
  2. Build a classification sheet per SKU (HS, CN and TARIC codes, origin) and check how many lines a typical basket creates under H7 and H1.
  3. Agree in writing who the declarant is. With indirect representation, you are a debtor too.
  4. Without IOSS, file in the Member State where delivery ends.
  5. For real EU stock, import before you sell and keep the chronology: purchase order, import declaration, warehouse receipt, then customer orders.
  6. Check the comprehensive guarantee and make sure its reference amount covers the new charges.
  7. Align product data: mandatory product identifiers should match your labels and GPSR file (see our article).

Common mistakes

  • Treating one pallet as one consignment when the boxes inside are already sold and labelled per customer.
  • Counting per parcel instead of per declaration line.
  • Assuming the handling fee stops at €150.
  • Expecting the charges back automatically when goods are returned.
  • Letting the broker choose the declaration and representation without knowing you may be a debtor.
  • Moving stock into the EU and forgetting that the product still needs an EU economic operator under GPSR.

Who this does not apply to

  • Sales to VAT-registered business customers: not distance sales, normal import rules apply.
  • Goods already in free circulation and sold from EU stock.
  • Customers who collect the goods or arrange transport without the seller’s involvement.
  • New means of transport and goods supplied with installation or assembly.
  • Consignments above €150: normal duty instead of €3, though the handling fee still applies to distance sales.
  • Excise and restricted goods: H1 rules, outside this article’s scope.

FAQ

Is the €3 duty charged per parcel or per product?

Per item, in practice per declaration line. Goods with the same tariff code, description and (where declared) origin share one line, whatever the quantity.

Is the €2 handling fee refunded if the customer returns the goods?

No. It is not refunded once customs has accepted the declaration. For the €3 duty, invalidation because goods up to €150 came back is no longer possible, although the general refund rules of the Union Customs Code still apply.

If I import stock into an EU warehouse first, do I still need an EU Responsible Person?

Yes, unless an economic operator established in the EU, such as an EU importer or your own EU company, takes on that role under GPSR. Moving stock into the EU does not by itself meet the requirement.

How long will the €3 duty apply?

Until 1 July 2028 as scheduled. By 1 December 2027 the Commission must assess whether the EU Customs Data Hub will be ready, and may propose an extension if not.
